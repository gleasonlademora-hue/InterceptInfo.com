Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about extreme storm intercept vehicles, their engineering, and how we track them.

InterceptInfo is a dedicated database tracking the world's most advanced storm intercept vehicles. We document their complex engineering, track their deployment history, and maintain live statuses of heavily modified machines built to survive extreme weather events.

The Tornado Intercept Vehicle 1 (TIV-1) was constructed by Sean Casey in 2003. Its primary mission was to safely transport an IMAX camera directly into the path of a low-level tornado to capture documentary footage.

No, the TIV-1 is no longer actively chasing storms and is currently listed in our Inactive Vehicles database.

Created by Reed Timmer, the Dominator 1 focuses strictly on aerodynamics rather than anchoring. It features a sloped outer shell that forces oncoming winds over the vehicle, effectively pinning it to the ground during a severe storm.

The Dominator 1 is heavily modified from a base 2007 Chevrolet Tahoe chassis.

Built to address weight and speed issues of its predecessor, the TIV-2 utilizes a full 4-side hydraulic skirt system that seals completely to the ground surface, along with 40-inch ground-penetrating steel spikes to anchor the vehicle against 200+ mph winds.

While TIV-2 was originally created and owned by Sean Casey, its current owner is Ryan Shepard.

The original TIV-1 weighs approximately 15,000 lbs (or 7.5 tons), making it the heaviest vehicle documented in our active or inactive databases.

Heavy custom trucks like the TIV or Dominator get very low gas mileage while idling for hours or driving thousands of miles across the plains. For example, the TIV-1 operates at about 8.3 mpg, while the Dominator 3 gets an estimated 1 mile per gallon during specific operations. You can help support this via our Tornado Alley Fuel Fund.

The Dominator 3, based on a massive 2013 Ford F-350 Super Duty chassis, features automated hydraulic gull-wing doors and a highly advanced air suspension system. When deployed, the entire vehicle lowers flush to the ground, eliminating underbody wind flow entirely.

Despite its size and 11,000 lbs weight, the Dominator 3 has a top speed of 80-110 mph (128.75-177.03 km/h).

Intercept vehicles take heavy hits from flying debris. Donations to this fund cover vital maintenance such as body repairs, replacing cracked bulletproof polycarbonate windshields, and performing hydraulic anchor maintenance.

Field communications are essential. Chasers require strong mobile satellite internet, rugged cameras, and upgraded heavy-duty signal antennas to safely broadcast data and video from inside the tornado core.

Vehicles are outfitted with atmospheric sensors, external anemometers, and deployable ground probe shells (turtle probes) to record crucial meteorological data directly inside the tornado.
