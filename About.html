About InterceptInfo

Have you ever wondered what it takes to drive straight into the heart of a monster? At InterceptInfo, we are endlessly fascinated by the intersection of extreme weather and unstoppable automotive engineering. We are the premier digital database dedicated exclusively to storm intercept vehicles, the heavily armored, purpose-built machines designed to withstand nature's most violent fury. Whether it is surviving wind speeds exceeding two hundred miles per hour or anchoring directly into the earth as a tornado passes overhead, these vehicles represent the pinnacle of meteorological research. Welcome to the ultimate hub for storm chasing engineering and history.

The Story & Origin

InterceptInfo began with a singular, awe-inspiring moment: watching the original Tornado Intercept Vehicle (TIV-1) capture groundbreaking IMAX footage from inside a twister. We realized that while the brave meteorologists and filmmakers inside these machines often receive the spotlight, the vehicles themselves are truly unsung heroes. They are rolling fortresses, meticulously crafted from heavy steel armor, bullet-resistant polycarbonate, and complex hydraulic systems. However, detailed specifications and historical data about these machines were scattered across the internet, buried in old forum posts, or lost in fragmented video descriptions. We created InterceptInfo to finally solve this problem. Our core mission was to build a centralized, easily accessible repository that honors the incredible legacy of these machines. From the pioneering days of early armored trucks to the modern era of aerodynamic interceptors that use the wind's own force to pin themselves to the ground, we wanted to document every weld, every window, and every deployable ground spike. What started as a small passion project has rapidly evolved into a comprehensive archive, uniting weather enthusiasts, engineering nerds, and scientific researchers under one digital roof to marvel at human ingenuity in the face of absolute atmospheric chaos.

Methodology & Curation

Compiling the data for InterceptInfo requires the same relentless dedication as tracking a supercell across the Great Plains. Our methodology relies on meticulous research, cross-referencing public records, and analyzing thousands of hours of chase footage. We meticulously categorize our database into active and inactive vehicles, providing real-time status updates on the machines currently roaming Tornado Alley versus those respectfully retired to museums. When curating technical specifications, we focus heavily on the core engineering elements that keep chasers alive. This includes documenting base chassis details, powertrain upgrades, horsepower output, and estimated wind threshold survivability. We closely examine armor materials, such as Line-X coatings, Kevlar layering, and thick Lexan glass, alongside specialized defense mechanisms like hydraulic skirts and ground-penetrating spikes. Furthermore, we track the scientific payload of each vehicle, from external anemometers to deployable ground probes designed to measure barometric pressure inside the core. Every single statistic is verified through historical build logs and creator interviews. We are constantly updating our live status database to ensure that when storm season peaks, our users have the most accurate, up-to-date information on the legendary interceptors currently deployed in the field.

Our Value Proposition

InterceptInfo offers unparalleled value by expertly bridging the gap between extreme meteorology and heavy-duty automotive engineering. For severe weather enthusiasts, we provide a definitive, one-stop resource to track the operational status and historical milestones of legendary interceptors. For engineering students and automotive builders, our detailed breakdowns of armor plating, aerodynamics, and structural reinforcements offer a unique masterclass in survival-focused design. Moreover, our platform directly supports the ongoing mission of scientific research. Through our dedicated donation portal, users can contribute directly to armor maintenance funds, fuel costs for high-risk intercept runs, and the replacement of critical scientific probes. By centralizing this data and facilitating community support, InterceptInfo not only educates the public but actively ensures that these essential vehicles remain safely on the road, gathering life-saving data for severe weather warning systems worldwide.

The Team Behind the Data

Behind the InterceptInfo project is a small, fiercely dedicated team of self-proclaimed weather nerds and mechanical enthusiasts. Based just outside Tornado Alley, our creators grew up watching storm chasers on television, captivated by the sheer bravery required to purposefully drive toward dark, rotating clouds. Our founder, a former automotive fabricator turned web developer, combined a lifelong love for custom car builds with a deep respect for meteorological science. We are not storm chasers ourselves, we happily leave the dangerous driving to the trained professionals, but we proudly serve as the ultimate fan club and historical archivist for those who do. Our team spends the off-season digging through obscure build threads and corresponding with original vehicle fabricators to ensure our database remains the gold standard, hoping to inspire future generations of engineers and atmospheric scientists.
